# Wikipedia-style article structure

Choose structure from the evidence and the page type. These are adaptable
patterns, not mandatory templates.

## General article

1. Lead
2. Background or definition
3. Development, history, or operation
4. Major aspects organized by theme
5. Reception, impact, criticism, or legacy when supported
6. References and relevant navigation

## Biography

1. Lead
2. Early life and education when well sourced and relevant
3. Career or public work divided into meaningful periods
4. Major work, positions, or contributions
5. Reception, awards, controversies, or legacy in proportion to sources
6. Personal life only where relevant and appropriately sourced

Do not force childhood, private life, or trivia into a biography because the
information is available.

## Organization or company

1. Lead
2. History and development
3. Products, services, operations, or structure
4. Financing, ownership, or governance when independently documented
5. Reception, impact, criticism, controversies, or legal matters in proportion
6. References

Avoid brochure structure such as mission, values, leadership list, product
catalog, and awards unless independent sources make those facts important.

## Event

1. Lead
2. Background
3. Event chronology or major components
4. Immediate response
5. Investigation, interpretation, or dispute
6. Consequences and longer-term significance

Separate what was known at the time from later findings.

## Technical or conceptual topic

1. Lead and concise definition
2. Background or origin
3. Mechanism, theory, or architecture
4. Variants, applications, or examples
5. Limitations, criticism, or competing approaches
6. History, adoption, or impact where supported

## Structural checks

- The lead and body describe the same article.
- Section weight follows source weight rather than stakeholder preference.
- Chronology is not repeated in several sections.
- Criticism is integrated by topic when a separate controversy section would
  distort proportion.
- Lists are used only when list structure helps the reader.
- Headings describe content plainly and avoid promotional language.
- Background stops where the subject itself begins.
- A short article is not inflated to imitate a mature one.
