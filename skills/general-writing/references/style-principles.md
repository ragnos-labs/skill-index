# General style principles

Use this reference for a full prose pass. Do not apply every principle
mechanically; protect good voice and intentional exceptions.

## Prefer

- concrete nouns and active verbs;
- a clear point early enough for the format;
- examples that carry information rather than decorate a claim;
- varied sentence and paragraph lengths that follow the thought;
- transitions that name the actual relationship between ideas;
- attributed judgments instead of anonymous consensus;
- specific stakes, tradeoffs, and consequences; and
- endings that resolve, decide, reveal, or point forward.

## Inspect closely

- broad claims of importance without evidence;
- canned openings such as announcing that a topic is important or complex;
- repeated restatements of the same idea;
- section symmetry that makes unrelated ideas feel templated;
- excessive headings, bullets, signposting, or summary sentences;
- vague authorities such as "experts say" or "studies show";
- generic intensifiers, inflated adjectives, and sales language;
- fake quotations or quotation marks used for emphasis;
- unnecessary parenthetical asides and stacked qualifications;
- abrupt fragments used only to simulate punchiness;
- uniform sentence cadence and repeated grammatical shapes;
- a conclusion that merely repeats the introduction; and
- public-facing text that exposes prompts, internal review notes, or production
  instructions.

## Source fidelity

When rewriting supplied material:

- keep names, dates, quantities, citations, links, and quotations attached to
  the claims they originally support;
- do not make a weak source sound stronger through confident phrasing;
- do not merge separate observations into a new causal claim;
- do not turn an allegation, estimate, plan, or opinion into a fact; and
- surface contradictions instead of choosing the more convenient version.

## Voice

Voice is more than word choice. Preserve the author's typical level of
directness, warmth, formality, humor, technical depth, pacing, and tolerance for
rough edges. A polished result can still sound like a person. Do not erase a
distinctive voice merely because a sentence differs from generic style advice.
