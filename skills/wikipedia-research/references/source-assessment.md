# Wikipedia source assessment

Use this reference to judge what a source can support. Do not reduce source
quality to a domain whitelist or a single score.

## Assessment dimensions

### Identity

Can the author, publisher, date, title, and original publication be identified?
Is the page the original source or a copy?

### Editorial control

Does the publisher have meaningful editorial review, fact-checking, correction,
or peer-review practices appropriate to the claim?

### Independence

Is the source organizationally and financially independent of the subject? A
source can be reliable for a narrow primary fact while remaining unsuitable as
independent evidence of importance.

### Depth

Does the source discuss the topic directly and in enough detail to support the
proposed material, or does it merely mention the name?

### Proximity

Is the source primary, secondary, or tertiary for this claim? Primary material
can establish what an organization said or did. Independent secondary sources
are usually stronger for interpretation, significance, and disputed context.

### Specificity

Does the cited passage directly support the proposed claim? Record an exact
page, paragraph, section, table, figure, or timestamp when possible.

### Currency and durability

Is the source current enough for the claim? For historical importance, does the
coverage persist beyond a short announcement or news cycle?

### Duplication

Does the source repeat a press release, wire story, database record, or another
article? Syndicated copies count as one reporting lineage, not several
independent sources.

### Conflict and bias

What incentives, affiliations, political positions, commercial relationships,
or methodological limitations affect how the source should be used?

## Source classes

- independent books, scholarship, and substantial journalism;
- reviews, profiles, and retrospective analysis;
- official records and public datasets;
- organization or subject websites, reports, interviews, and press releases;
- trade publications and specialist outlets;
- databases, directories, event listings, and routine announcements;
- self-published commentary and social posts;
- mirrors, aggregators, syndication, and unattributed summaries.

The class informs judgment but does not decide it. Match the source to the
claim.

## Coverage judgment

Report one of:

- **Strong**: multiple independent sources provide substantial, direct,
  complementary coverage.
- **Mixed**: useful independent coverage exists, but important sections depend
  on affiliated, shallow, dated, or repetitive sources.
- **Weak**: available material is mostly primary, promotional, trivial,
  syndicated, or unable to support a balanced full article.
- **Unknown**: the search was too limited, inaccessible, or language-bound to
  support a judgment.

This judgment describes the evidence available to the sample. It is not a
prohibition on creating a private learning artifact.
