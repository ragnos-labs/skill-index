# Writing review rubric

Score each applicable dimension from 0 to 4. Scores organize judgment; the
written evidence matters more than the arithmetic.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Missing, unusable, or seriously misleading |
| 1 | Major problems dominate |
| 2 | Mixed result with material repair needed |
| 3 | Strong and usable with bounded improvements |
| 4 | Excellent for the stated purpose and evidence |

## Dimensions

### Purpose

The piece has a discernible job, prioritizes it, and ends in a way appropriate
to that job.

### Audience

Context, terminology, tone, detail, examples, and assumptions fit the intended
reader.

### Evidence

Claims match the supplied facts and sources. Quotations, citations, numbers,
and uncertainty are accurate and appropriately represented.

### Structure

The order builds understanding or persuasion. Sections have distinct jobs,
proportions reflect importance, and repetition is purposeful.

### Clarity

Sentences make relationships explicit, terms are stable, pronouns resolve, and
the reader rarely needs to reread.

### Specificity

Claims use concrete actors, actions, examples, quantities, mechanisms, or
consequences where the evidence permits.

### Voice

The writing sounds deliberate and consistent with the author or brand without
becoming generic, theatrical, or over-polished.

### Economy

Every section earns its space. Signposting, throat-clearing, summaries,
qualifiers, and examples are proportionate.

### Publication readiness

Formatting, links, citations, captions, metadata, calls to action, and internal
production residue fit the destination.

## Overall judgment

Report both:

1. **Readiness**: ready, ready with minor edits, needs revision, or rebuild.
2. **Priority**: the one repair that would improve the piece most.

Never convert the dimensional scores into a claim about who wrote the text or
whether a model was used.
