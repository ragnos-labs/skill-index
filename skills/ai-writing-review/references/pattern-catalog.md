# AI-like writing pattern catalog

This catalog supports editorial judgment. A single pattern is not evidence of
AI authorship, and many patterns also occur in rushed, corporate, academic, or
formulaic human writing.

## Generic framing

- announces that a topic is important, complex, evolving, or transformative
  without showing why;
- opens with a universal claim unrelated to the specific reader;
- uses abstract scene-setting before naming the subject;
- relies on phrases such as "in today's landscape" or "it is worth noting";
- claims something is a testament, reminder, pivotal moment, or game changer
  without evidence.

## Mechanical organization

- every section has the same length and internal pattern;
- the introduction previews every heading and the conclusion repeats them;
- bullets replace relationships that require explanation;
- excessive headings fragment a simple thought;
- each paragraph begins with a transition and ends with a mini-summary;
- a forced three-part structure appears regardless of the material.

## Synthetic rhythm

- sentences repeat the same length, syntax, or opening;
- short fragments are inserted only to simulate confidence: "The result?";
- em dashes, semicolons, or parentheticals appear at a mechanical cadence;
- paired contrasts recur: "not only X, but Y" or "it is not X; it is Y";
- rhetorical questions are immediately answered without adding tension;
- triplets and parallel clauses accumulate beyond what the argument needs.

## Vague or inflated language

- actors disappear behind passive voice or nominalizations;
- authorities are unnamed: experts, observers, research, many people;
- verbs are replaced by abstractions such as leveraging, fostering, navigating,
  unlocking, elevating, or driving impact;
- modifiers advertise importance rather than provide information;
- familiar words are replaced with grand or unusual synonyms;
- the piece remains positive and polished even where the evidence is mixed.

## Unsupported texture

- quotations, testimonials, examples, or statistics have no source;
- sensory or biographical details appear despite not existing in the brief;
- a transition invents causality, intention, consensus, or chronology;
- precise-looking facts make a passage feel credible but cannot be verified;
- source limitations disappear during a rewrite.

## Meta-writing and public residue

- the text announces what the article will discuss instead of discussing it;
- the writer refers to the prompt, requested tone, word count, sources, or
  drafting process in public copy;
- placeholders, bracketed instructions, or internal review notes survive;
- the ending offers to provide more information or asks what to write next.

## Overcorrection risks

Cleaning can make a piece worse when it:

- removes intentional repetition, technical precision, or a real brand phrase;
- treats every common transition as forbidden;
- replaces clear words with quirky ones;
- adds slang, errors, jokes, anecdotes, or contractions without voice evidence;
- breaks logical paragraphs merely to vary length;
- deletes citations or qualifications for a cleaner rhythm; or
- optimizes for detector scores instead of the reader.

## Review rule

Name the observable pattern, quote the affected passage, explain its reader
impact, and propose the smallest useful repair. Avoid unsupported claims about
the writer or the production process.
